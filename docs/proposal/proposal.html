1. The problem

Every business has the same recurring moment: an opportunity appears and someone has to show up prepared with a tailored offer. A discovery call lands in the calendar. A lead emails. An investor agrees to a coffee. A tender is published. The work behind each is identical and it is done from scratch every time.

  1. Know yourself. What do we actually offer, at what price, with what proof? The answer lives in scattered slide decks, rate cards and old proposals.
  2. Know them. Who is this prospect, what are they doing, where are they stuck? Usually twenty minutes of browser tabs, or nothing at all.
  3. Find the fit. Which of our things solves which of their gaps, and what would we customise? This is the step that wins or loses the deal, and it happens in someone's head.
  4. Make the ask. A brief for the meeting, then a proposal, quote, deck or reply. Hours of writing that starts from last quarter's document.
  5. Close the loop. Follow up, and learn from whether it was won or lost. Almost nobody does this systematically.

Who feels it most

Large enterprises have sales engineers, bid teams and a CRM full of AI features. Most companies in the JAPAC region do not. In SMEs, startups and specialist integrators, what the company sells lives in scattered decks, spreadsheets and people's heads, and selling is done by the same people who deliver the work. Preparation competes with billable hours, knowledge leaves with the people who leave, and offers go out generic.

The gap in today's tools. Each step has its own product category: meeting-prep tools that produce a brief but know nothing about what you sell; RFP suites that know your content library but only speak tender; proposal writers that format a document from a template; roleplay tools built for training sales development reps. All of them assume you already live in a CRM. Nobody gives a company one grounded source of truth for what it sells, and then uses it to drive all five steps.

2. The solution

FlowHub is the hub for everything a company sells, with an agent team built in. The hub is where staff find everything the company sells. It is an offering graph of offerings, tiers, rate cards, case studies, constraints and partners, extracted from the documents the company already has, with every item linked to its source page. The deal room is each opportunity's page in the hub, where a team of agents works from the hub and a person approves every stage.

The user's journey

  1. Set up the hub. Upload the deck, the rate card and a few past proposals. Gemini reads them, including scanned PDFs, and proposes the catalogue. The team confirms it in a few minutes and invites colleagues as Editors or Viewers.
  2. Open an opportunity. Paste a calendar invite, an email, a company URL or a tender PDF. The Intake agent classifies it and extracts who, what and when.
  3. Read the brief. The Researcher agent produces a one-page brief on the prospect with every claim linked to its source: what they do, recent news, hiring signals, technology signals, and the sector's typical pressures.
  4. Review the fit map. The Fit Analyst matches each identified gap to an item in the offering graph, proposes customisations, flags what cannot be met, and gives a go/no-go score with reasoning. The Pricing agent adds an indicative estimate from the rate cards.
  5. Approve the artefact. The Writer produces the persona-appropriate output as a Google Doc or Slides file. The Reviewer agent checks it for unsupported claims before the human sees it.
  6. Ask or rehearse. Any member can ask the hub a question and get an answer cited to the catalogue. Before the meeting, the seller can rehearse out loud with Gemini Live playing the prospect.
  7. Record the outcome. Won, lost, or no decision, with a reason. FlowHub adjusts what it emphasises for that sector next time.

4. Architecture

FlowHub is built entirely on Google Cloud. The web application runs on Cloud Run. The agents are written with the Agent Development Kit (ADK) and hosted on Agent Runtime in Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (formerly Vertex AI Agent Engine). All state lives in Firestore, which also serves vector search for the offering graph. Documents are stored in Cloud Storage and read by Gemini directly.

The offering graph

Ingestion turns unstructured sales material into typed entities: Offering (product or service, with its features), RateCard (tiers or packages against line items), FeeSchedule (one-time, recurring and variable charges), Proof (case study, metric, logo), Constraint (what we do not do), and Partner. Past proposals are kept as source documents. Each entity, down to a single price cell, keeps a link to the page or slide it came from, so the Fit Analyst can cite the seller's own material the same way the Researcher cites the web. Gemini extracts entities with a JSON schema through structured output, and the user confirms or edits them in a review screen before they are used.

Request flow for one opportunity

  1. The web app writes the opportunity to Firestore and starts an Agent Runtime session.
  2. The Orchestrator runs Intake, then Researcher. Progress streams back to the deal room through Firestore listeners.
  3. The human reads the brief and presses Continue. The Fit Analyst retrieves offerings by vector search and maps each gap. The Pricing agent then selects rate-card lines through function calls, and code copies the prices, so the model never writes a figure.
  4. The human edits the fit map, then Writer produces the artefact and Reviewer critiques it. If Reviewer finds an unsupported claim, Writer revises, up to two loops.
  5. The artefact link, the review report and the go/no-go score are saved on the opportunity. Rehearsal and outcome capture are available from here.

Controls against hallucination

  • Research claims are only accepted with a grounding source; ungrounded sentences are dropped at the Researcher stage, not hidden.
  • Prices and offerings come from function calls into the offering graph, never from the model's memory.
  • The Reviewer scores each claim in the artefact as supported by a research source, an offering entity, or neither. "Neither" blocks the artefact until the human accepts or removes it.
  • ADK evaluation sets with ten sample opportunities run in CI to catch regressions in fit quality.

Scalability

Every service is stateless and scales to zero: Cloud Run for the app, Agent Runtime for the agents, Firestore for state and vectors. Workspaces are isolated by Firestore security rules and by a workspace filter on every vector query. Adding a persona is a new intake parser and output template; adding a language is a prompt change, since Gemini handles JAPAC languages natively.

Beyond the hackathon

FlowHub is priced per workspace, with a free tier for small teams. The hub becomes more valuable with every document, proposal and outcome recorded, which is the retention mechanism. Tenders, which Trassoq answers in the demo, extend naturally into public procurement portals across the region.
